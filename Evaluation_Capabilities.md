# Evaluation Capabilities

## Purpose

The evaluation component reviews AI-generated responses as analytical artifacts. It is intended to help identify where an answer is correct, incomplete, unsupported, contradicted, or misleading.

## Capability Areas

### Factual and Regulatory Accuracy

The evaluator can compare dates, populations, product descriptions, regulatory claims, and reported figures against authoritative evidence when that evidence is available.

### Claim Decomposition

Complex answers can be separated into individual claims so that a response is not accepted or rejected as one undifferentiated block.

### Evidence Quality

The design prioritizes primary and authoritative sources, compares source relevance and dates, and distinguishes evidence inspected during a test from material added during later review.

### Numerical and Logical Consistency

The evaluator can examine whether numbers are used with the correct denominator, population, endpoint, comparison, and interpretation. It can also detect conclusions that do not follow from the cited result.

### Misleading Interpretation Detection

The Leqembi case illustrates the distinction between a relative slowing of decline and an improvement in cognitive function. The ANKTIVA case illustrates the distinction between complete response and cure.

### Contradiction and Alternative-Explanation Review

For more consequential research, the design supports looking for contradictory evidence, alternative explanations, weak assumptions, and factors that could change the conclusion. This is a capability description, not a disclosed internal procedure.

### Uncertainty Management

The evaluator is expected to identify missing source access, incomplete traces, unreadable documents, ambiguous attribution, and other limitations rather than conceal them.

### Transparent Reporting

Useful output separates findings, evidence, interpretations, and limitations. It should make clear which conclusions were produced during the original test and which were confirmed later by a reviewer.

## Demonstrated Versus Designed

The two case studies demonstrate claim correction, evidence-aware reasoning, and limitation reporting. They do not prove repeatable accuracy across a broad dataset, complete traceability, measured resource efficiency, or comprehensive security isolation.
